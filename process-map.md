# Patient Attack Plan — Step by Step

**Goal:** Turn "I can't get the treatment I deserve" into a documented, multi-channel pressure campaign that the system has to respond to.

## Stage 0 — Before you start (the foundation)
- Keep a running timeline: date, who, what was said, what was promised, what happened instead.
- Save every email, portal message, after-visit summary, lab result, and note.
- Request your full medical records in writing (you have a right to them).
- Note any deadlines (OCR privacy complaints: 180 days from when you knew).

## Stage 1 — Internal (the facility)
- Ask for the **Patient Advocate** or **Patient Relations** department. Not the front desk.
- Submit the complaint **in writing**. Request a written response and a timeline.
- Under CMS rules (42 CFR 482.13), hospitals must investigate and respond in writing.
- Get a reference number or confirmation of receipt.
- If they stonewall or give a vague answer, move to Stage 2.

## Stage 2 — Parallel external escalation
You do not have to wait for one to finish before starting another. Hit them together.

### A. The Doctor (State Medical Board)
- Find your state's board: fsmb.org (Federation of State Medical Boards directory)
- File online complaint. Upload your timeline and key records.
- This triggers an investigation into licensing. Slow but real.

### B. The Facility (Hospital / Clinic)
- Patient Advocate / Grievance department (internal, but formal).
- State Department of Health (facility licensing).
- CMS / state survey agency if Medicare/Medicaid certified.

### C. Accreditation (The Joint Commission)
- Only if the facility is TJC-accredited.
- Online form: jointcommission.org (they shred medical records — summarize, don't attach PHI).
- Phone: 1-800-994-6610
- Can trigger an unannounced survey.

### D. Civil Rights / Privacy (HHS OCR)
- For HIPAA violations, discrimination, or patient rights breaches.
- Portal: ocrportal.hhs.gov
- 180-day window for privacy complaints.
- Written response required.

### E. Medicare (if applicable)
- BFCC-QIO for your state (quality of care).
- Find via medicare.gov.

### F. Fraud / Billing (HHS OIG)
- If billing fraud suspected: 1-800-HHS-TIPS or oig.hhs.gov

## Stage 3 — The lever (what makes them move)
- **Documentation density.** A clean timeline with dates, names, and quotes is harder to dismiss than a rant.
- **Multiple channels.** One complaint is easy to bury. Five is a pattern.
- **Reference numbers.** Track every one. Follow up in writing.
- **Supervisor escalation.** Ask the person you're talking to: "Do you have a supervisor? Can you make notes of this conversation and send them to me?" Then send the notes to the supervisor yourself.
- **Public record.** Some boards publish disciplinary actions. Your complaint becomes part of the file.

## Stage 4 — If it still fails
- Consult a medical malpractice attorney (many offer free consults; statutes of limitations vary by state, often 1-3 years).
- Consider a formal grievance with the state health department.
- Document the failure to respond — that itself can be a complaint.

## The golden rule
Never stop at "I don't know." Ask for the supervisor, the policy, the written response. The system is built to wear you down. Your job is to make the paperwork do the wearing.
